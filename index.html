I nudge only when I'm sure.

Mirrors the Oct 26, 2026 launch of Lakeflow Performance Observability (GA): job-level query performance metrics plus an Optimize button that opens Genie Code. The hard call: when should a job page wave that Optimize nudge at you instead of waiting to be clicked?

Shriya Shekatkar UCLA · Stats 102C Monte Carlo Methods Method: Bayesian Beta-Binomial (Ch2)

The decision

A performance page is only useful if people trust its alarms. Badge too eagerly and data engineers learn to ignore it; badge too late and a slow job burns compute for weeks.

Customer problem

"Which of my 200 jobs actually got slower?"

Run times are noisy. One slow run can be a cold cluster, a skewed input day, or a real regression. Owners don't have time to open every job, so they need the product to point at the few that truly drifted.

Proposed change

A proactive "Optimize" badge

Show a badge on the job list (and one digest notification per week) only when the posterior probability that a job's slow-run rate is above 20% clears a confidence bar τ. A "slow run" is one that takes more than 1.5× the job's trailing median.

Success metric

Badge precision

Share of badged jobs that were truly regressed. If people click a badge and find nothing wrong, the feature loses its credibility fast. Secondary: Optimize-button click-through from badged jobs.

The math, from my Monte Carlo course

Chapter 2 of Stats 102C: treat the unknown as something with a distribution, and let evidence reshape it.

Frequentist vs Bayesian, in product words

A frequentist says each job has one fixed slow-run rate and estimates it by the raw fraction: 2 slow out of 5 runs is 40%. With five runs that number swings wildly. A Bayesian says "I'm uncertain about the rate" and keeps a whole distribution for it, so 2 of 5 reads as "maybe high, not proven".

Beta prior + Binomial runs = Beta posterior

rate ~ Beta(a, b)
slow runs s of n ~ Binomial(n, rate)
⇒ rate | s ~ Beta(a + s, b + n − s)

Conjugacy means updating is just adding counts. My default prior Beta(0.4, 3.6) is worth 4 pseudo-runs at a 10% slow rate: a gentle nudge, not a wall.

A weighted average you can explain

Posterior mean = (a + s)/(a + b + n). It sits between the prior mean (10%) and the data mean (s/n), weighted by the prior's pseudo-runs vs n real runs. Crank the prior-strength slider to 40 and the prior dominates; drop it to 1 and the data does. A flat Beta(1,1) prior makes the most likely value equal to the raw fraction.

Why Monte Carlo on top?

The badge rule needs P(rate > 20%), a tail area of the posterior. I estimate it the Chapter 1 way: draw 400 rates from each job's posterior and count the share above 20%. The count is Binomial, so the error shrinks like 1/√400 ≈ 2.5 points. That's plenty to tell 0.6 from 0.95.

Credible intervals for the job page

The 5th–95th percentile of the draws becomes the tooltip: "This job is slow on 18–47% of runs." That's a direct probability statement about this job, which an owner can act on, unlike a confidence interval's coverage promise about repeated experiments.

How I'd run it on real data

Illustrative only: table and column names are made up to show the shape of the pipeline.

1 · Count slow runs per job (SQL)

WITH runs AS (
  SELECT job_id, run_id, duration_s,
    percentile(duration_s, 0.5) OVER (
      PARTITION BY job_id ORDER BY start_ts
      ROWS BETWEEN 30 PRECEDING AND 11 PRECEDING
    ) AS base_med,
    ROW_NUMBER() OVER (
      PARTITION BY job_id ORDER BY start_ts DESC
    ) AS rn
  FROM ops.lab.job_run_history
  WHERE start_ts >= current_date() - INTERVAL 30 DAYS
)
SELECT job_id,
  COUNT(*) AS n_runs,
  SUM(IF(duration_s > 1.5 * base_med, 1, 0))
    AS n_slow
FROM runs
WHERE rn <= 10 AND base_med IS NOT NULL
GROUP BY job_id;

2 · Posterior tail + badge (Python)

import numpy as np
rng = np.random.default_rng(102)
A, B, CUT, TAU = 0.4, 3.6, 0.20, 0.80

df = spark.table("ops.lab.job_slow_counts").toPandas()
draws = rng.beta(A + df.n_slow.values[:, None],
                 B + (df.n_runs - df.n_slow).values[:, None],
                 size=(len(df), 400))
df["p_slow"] = (draws > CUT).mean(axis=1)
df["ci_lo"], df["ci_hi"] = np.percentile(draws, [5, 95], axis=1)
df["badge"] = df.p_slow >= TAU

(spark.createDataFrame(df)
   .write.mode("overwrite")
   .saveAsTable("ops.lab.optimize_badges"))
